Throughout my life I have always been drawn to nature and learning about how everything around me works – the wind, the clouds, the trees, the Earth, and especially the oceans. At the age of 12, once I saw the world beneath the ocean waves, my heart felt something that my mind couldn’t understand quite yet. At the same time, because of my father, I grew up looking at the world through numbers. All these late nights over the algebra books have definitely left their mark on me. At first glance numbers might look simple without any evident pattern, but if you just look closer, you might find secrets hidden underneath the surface – that’s why I decided to pursue Computer Science, Economics with a strong focus in Statistics. Some of my hobbies are hiking, especially camping in the mountains, and surfing.

I also love hearing people’s life stories and learning about their experiences. There are so many people in the world, and every person has such a unique story and perspective. I find that really fascinating.

In the summer of 2026 I was a marine technician intern at CWORI in the U.S. Virgin Islands. I built 2D and 3D models of reefs from underwater photos, used them to measure coral cover, mortality and survival, and helped restore reefs in the water: collecting wild coral, fragmenting colonies and outplanting the fragments onto reef sites.

At Grinnell College, my academic work has explored the intersection of economics, climate change, and environmental systems. My research has examined the economic impacts of climate change on marine ecosystems and natural resources, including natural gas and oil, as well as the relationship between corruption and economic development in major economies. Alongside my economics coursework, I have built an interdisciplinary foundation in environmental science, studying ocean systems and their interactions with the climate and atmosphere.
