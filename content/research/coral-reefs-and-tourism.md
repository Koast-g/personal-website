Our study investigates the critical economic impact of coral reef degradation on coastal countries, specifically focusing on tourism revenue. Due to ongoing climate change, coral reefs are under severe pressure and their decline, which is measured by recorded hot spots in the ocean in degrees Celsius, creates a threat to the marine tourism sector. We use data from National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration from the past 25 years to examine whether there is a relationship between coral degradation and tourism revenue. Our results do not provide strong evidence that increases in coral bleaching severity reduce national tourism revenue, even though the point estimates are generally negative.
